Project Overview

Finncare is an Australian aged-care organisation providing residential aged care, retirement living, and home support services, with strong connections to the Australian Finnish community.

The organisation already had an existing website , and our project focused on creating an improved version of the site based on requirements and feedback provided by the product owner.

Rather than building the website from scratch, we are redesigning and modifying the existing digital experience to make the website more modern, accessible, user-friendly, and easier to maintain through WordPress.

Project Goals

The main goals of the redesign are to:

  1. Modernise the overall website design.
  2. Improve navigation and information hierachy.
  3. Make important services easier for visitors to discover.
  4. Improve readability and accessibility, particularly for an older target audience.
  5. Create a responsive experience acrosss desktop, tablet, and mobile devices.
  6. Implement the updated design using WordPress.
  7. Ensure the website remains easy for the organisation to update and maintain.

Learning Reflection - English for Communication in Customer Projects

Learning through the Finncare project

The Finncare website redesign gave me an opportunity to connect English communication with a practical IT project. My role includes Scrum Master as well as Web Developer, so the project gave me opportunities to think about both teamwork and website development.

Our goal was to improve Finncare's existing website by making it easier to navigate, read, and use on different devices. Since older people are an important target audience, we also need to consider accessibility and how easily visitors could find information about services.

The project helped me see that website development involves much more than creating pages. We needed to understand the product owner's expectations, discuss ideas as a team, and turn requirements into practical tasks. Clear communication was important throughout this process.

Using English in the project

One of the main lessons for me was the importance of checking that everyone understands the same thing. A request such as "make the website easier to use" can mean different things to different people. We need to ask questions before deciding what to change.

I learned that I don't need complicated English to communicate professionally. A simple, clear sentences can be more useful, especially when explaining a design choice to someone without a technical background. Asking questions such as "Could you explain that a little more?" or "Do you mean that we should change the navigation?" can help to avoid confusion.

My Scrum Master role also made me think more carefully about communication within the team. Team members need to know what they are working on, what has been completed, and where help is needed. Listening to other and giving them space to explain their ideas are skills I want to keep developing.

Learning project vocabulary

The project helped me connect English IT vocabulary with practical work. Terms such as product backlog, sprint, stakeholder, prototype, accessibility, and usability became easier to understand when I could connect them to our activities.

For example, organising requirements into a backlog showed me how a customer's request becomes a task for the team. Working with prototypes also showed me how a visual design can make an idea easier to discuss.

Using Jira and Confluence highlighted the importance of clear writing. A task should contain enough information for another person to understand what needs to be done. This made me think about how I organise information and whether my descriptions would make sense to someone reading them for the first time.

What was challenging

Explaining technical ideas clearly is an area I want to improve. Understanding an idea myself and explaining it to another person are different skills. I need to think about which details matter and choose words that suit the person I am speaking to.

In future, i would like to prepare few key points beforehand. This would help me to organise my thoughts and explain my ideas more confidently. I also want to ask for clarification sooner when something is unclear, rather than spend time working from an assumption.

What I am satisfied with

One rewarding part of the project was seeing how the website ideas developed through different stages. Reviewing the existing website, exploring prototypes, refining designs in Figma, and planning the WordPress work helped me understand the overall process.

I also found it useful to think about the website from the visitor's point of view. A design may look attractive, but people still need to find the information they came for. This helped me understand why clear headings, readable text, and straightforward navigation matter.

My progress and next steps:

The Finncare project has given me practical situations to reflect on during the English for Communication in Customer Projects course. It helped me to connect communication with understanding requirements, orgianising teamwork, expaining decisions, and documenting work.

My next goal is to feel more comfortable explaining design decisions and answering questions in English. I also want to improve how I write task descriptions and project documentation. As the project is still in progress, I will continue adding examples of what went well, what was difficult, and what I would do differently next time.

Development approach

The project follows an iterative Agile workflow. We first familiarised ourselves with Finncare, the existing website, and the product owner's expectations. We then translated the requirements into a product backlog and organised the development work into sprints.

01 - Understanding the project

we reviewed the existing Finncare website and discussed the project goals, target users, requirements, and expectations with the product owner.

02 - Teams roles and planning

We defined responsibilities within the project team and planned how we would collaborate throughout the development process.

03 - Product backlog

We converted requirements into tasks and user-focused improvements and organised them in the product backlog usig Jira and Confluence.

04 - Initial prototype

Before implementation, we explored ideas through an early hand-drawn prototype to establish the structure and direction of the redesigned website.

05 - Figma Design

We refined the above obtained prototpe in Figma and developed the redesigned website in WordPress. During this stage, we refined the layout, navigation, page structure, typography, spacing, and overall user experience.

06 - Sprint development

The selected backlog items were organised into sprint backlogs such as Sprint 1, Sprint 2 and Sprint 3 and progressively implemented in WordPress.

07 - WordPress Development

After finalising the design direction, we implemented the redesigned website in WordPress and adapted the layouts for desktop, tablet, and mobile devices.

08 - User testing

We tested the website with users to identify usablility issues and collect feedback that could inform further improvements.
